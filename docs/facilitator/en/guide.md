# The game, workshop mode and the six acts

This guide is for anyone who plays Le Jardin with a group: a teacher, a youth worker, a community gardener. It covers how the game teaches, how to prepare the devices, what each of the six acts is for, the solutions to all eleven challenges, questions for the closing conversation and plans for 45, 90 and 120 minutes.

Le Jardin is a free browser game about designing a living permaculture garden, inspired by a real community garden in Menet, France. It needs no account and keeps the garden in the browser of the device it is played on. It is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Erasmus+.

## How the game teaches

The player builds a garden out of **relationships**, not a collection of objects. Elements placed close together can form a *design connection* (rain harvesting beside a herb spiral, for example), wild neighbours arrive when the garden offers what they need, and four living layers (soil, water, food, habitat) grow stronger as they start to answer one another.

The guided journey leads through **six acts**. The first four describe the garden as it is now: an act is done while the garden really has that layer, and taking the element away undoes it. The fifth asks for a gift, the sixth for a turn of the season and one garden challenge.

Some rules hold everywhere in the game, and they are worth saying out loud to a group:

- **No timers and no penalties.** A wrong answer in a challenge gives a clue and another try. A challenge can also be left for later; it comes back.
- **Gifts never turn into personal money.** *Shares* are participation tokens, earned by noticing connections and by taking part. A gift to the community table strengthens the shared fund and goodwill, and it never buys anything back.
- **The AI never decides.** The optional *Living Lesson* names relationships in the garden and asks one question. Rules, points and outcomes always come from the game itself. In workshop mode the Living Lesson uses only the game's reviewed offline lessons.
- **The game simplifies.** Its facts carry the uncertainty they have in real gardens ("may", "can support"). For real planting, local conditions decide.

## Workshop mode

Give the group a link with `?workshop=1`. It works in every language of the game:

- English: <https://permaculture-garden.vercel.app/?workshop=1&lang=en>
- Czech: <https://permaculture-garden.vercel.app/?workshop=1&lang=cs>
- French: <https://permaculture-garden.vercel.app/?workshop=1&lang=fr>

What changes with the workshop link:

- **A garden of its own.** The workshop garden is saved apart from anyone's own game on the device. Players' own gardens are never read, changed or cleared.
- **A choice of where to begin.** The opening card asks *Where does this group begin?*: with the opening story (about one minute) or at any of the six acts. A later act starts with the earlier acts already done, using the plainest elements, placed far apart so that every design connection is still left for the group to find. Add `&act=3` to the link to preselect the third act on every device.
- **The same lesson for every group.** The Living Lesson answers only from the game's offline lessons: no AI, no cost, no network request.
- **A panel for the facilitator.** The *Workshop* button in the header (in the ••• menu on a phone) replaces *Reset*. It opens *For the facilitator*: where this group is, a question to ask, the six acts with their goals, the session log, this guide, and *Prepare for the next group*.
- **A session log.** The device notes what the group does: when it began, each act completed, challenges solved (and how many other ways the group tried first), seasons, design connections, wild neighbours, gifts, Living Lesson experiments and certificates. It records no names and nothing a player writes. *Save as a file* gives a plain text file; *Print* prints the log on its own. Nothing in the log is sent anywhere.

## Before the session

- **Devices.** One device for two or three players works best; one per player is fine too. A laptop or tablet gives the most room; phones work in portrait and landscape. Charge everything.
- **Connection.** Open the workshop link on every device before the group arrives. Where the signal is weak, choose *Play without a connection* on each device beforehand (the arrow in the header; on a phone, in the ••• menu): the download is about 20–35 MB, depending on whether you keep the Garden's voice and the music, and afterwards the game, the workshop link included, opens and plays without a connection. Other languages and the moving pictures still need one.
- **Language.** Choose it on the opening card or in the header. The garden speaks in English and Czech; in the other languages, including French, players read the text and hear the English voice.
- **Sound.** In a shared room, use headphones or switch the sound off with the speaker button in the header.
- **Start point.** Decide where the groups begin. Starting at a later act saves time and leads straight to the topic you want to discuss (see the plans below).
- **A pause.** The game saves itself after every step. A group can close the tab and continue later on the same device with the same link.
- **Between groups.** *Workshop* → save or print the log if you want it → *Prepare for the next group* → confirm. The device returns to the opening card, ready to start where the last group did.

## The six acts

### First act · Living soil

**Goal.** Living soil comes first: organic matter and cover feed the life that feeds every plant.

**In the game.** *Begin with my soil*: choose compost or mulch and place it where the bare ground needs care. The act is done while the garden has compost, mulch or a hügelkultur bed.

**Ask the group.** What lives in the soil, and what does it need from us before anything else can grow? What happens to bare soil on a hot, dry day?

**Common misconceptions.** Soil is only a place to hold plants. More digging is always better. The game rewards cover and a light touch.

**Tip.** At first the guided journey offers only compost and mulch. Before the group opens every tool, ask why the garden might want to start there.

### Second act · Every drop

**Goal.** Rain is caught, slowed and stored, so it serves the garden instead of rushing away.

**In the game.** *Catch and keep water*: add rain harvesting, then place a growing area nearby so the stored water has somewhere useful to go. The act is done while the garden has rain harvesting.

**Ask the group.** Where does the rain on this garden go now, and where could it pause and be useful? Why does the barrel have a lid?

**Common misconceptions.** Water from a roof is drinking water: it is not, and the game says so. A barrel alone solves the water question: the overflow needs a safe place to soak in.

**Tip.** When an element is selected, the plot marks circles where it would form a relationship. Placing it inside one makes the relationship visible.

### Third act · Useful abundance

**Goal.** Food grows in layers and partnerships, not as single crops standing alone.

**In the game.** *Grow food in layers*: add an edible bed, a fruit tree, a herb spiral or a planted arch. A seed bank can prepare crops, but the act is done when food is growing.

**Ask the group.** Which of your plants does more than one job, and what could you place beside it?

**Common misconceptions.** One useful pair of plants is a full plant guild: the game keeps the two apart, a pair is only a beginning. A garden with more objects is a better garden.

**Tip.** Compare two groups' gardens: who found a design connection, and how?

### Fourth act · Room for others

**Goal.** A resilient garden leaves room for wild neighbours, which need shelter and water as well as food.

**In the game.** *Make a home for wildlife*: plant a flower strip or add a bug hotel or a bird house.

**Ask the group.** Who else lives in this garden, and what does each of them need besides food?

**Common misconceptions.** Wild animals are pests, or rewards, or workers. A bug hotel guarantees residents: the game says "may", because nothing is guaranteed.

**Tip.** *Who might visit your garden* shows what each wild neighbour needs. Ask the group to read one out.

### Fifth act · Fair share

**Goal.** Fair share: a genuine surplus circulates as a gift and never turns into personal currency.

**In the game.** *Let one gift circulate*: pick a glowing cherry cluster and share it from the harvest basket at the community table. Later a genuine spare can go too: a second, established copy of an element, or finished compost, mulch or herb cuttings from one that has been through a season. The community table opens at this act.

**Ask the group.** What can the garden give away without becoming poorer, and why is a gift not a purchase here?

**Common misconceptions.** Shares are money. A gift should earn points. Sharing means giving away what the garden itself needs.

**Tip.** Point out the shared fund: it carries the whole garden through a bad year, and nobody owns it.

### Sixth act · Learn through change

**Goal.** Seasons change the garden; observe the whole system before acting on one symptom.

**In the game.** *Turn to the next season*: press *Next season* above the plot. A challenge meets the group there; solving it completes the act and leaves a lasting effect in the garden.

**Ask the group.** What changed with the season, and which other parts of the garden does that change reach?

**Common misconceptions.** A wrong answer is a failure: it is a clue. One quick fix solves a problem everywhere: the challenges ask what solves today's problem without creating tomorrow's.

**Tip.** The solutions to every challenge are on the next pages. Let the group try first; use the solution only to unblock.

### After the six acts

*Reflect on my garden* shows what the group has made and what remains. The seasons go on: a garden that has been through a full year, keeps every living layer at 45% or more, keeps two design connections active, has shared two gifts, harvested something and learned from three different challenges reaches the flourishing ending. There the group can write a name and save its **certificate of learning** as an image or print it. In a short session few groups get that far, and they do not need to: the six acts are the learning journey.
